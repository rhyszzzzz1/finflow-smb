1. Introduction

Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (SMEs) are the main source of most national economies; nevertheless, they always face the challenge of effective management of their finances. Typical issues are disjointed accounting databases, absence of real-time financial transparency, ineffective inventory management, slow receivable collections, and overdependence on Excel or manual bookkeeping systems. These issues often result in cash-flow stress, miscalculations of tax, and poor financial decision-making.

Innovations in cloud computing have made it possible to create cloud-based accounting applications that are highly automated, accessible, and integrated at relatively low cost. Although these advantages are real, many SMEs remain reluctant to embrace such systems, citing data safety, complexity, regulatory compliance, and technical insufficiency.

This literature review analyses academic and industry literature critically to gain insight on:

  • the adoption factors of cloud accounting for SMEs;
  • the operational value of integrating inventory with accounting systems;
  • the role of receivables and cash-flow management;
  • security and privacy concerns in cloud platforms; and
  • usability and spreadsheet-based data onboarding through CSV.

The review provides academic groundwork for the design and implementation of FinTrac, the proposed cloud-based SME accounting and business management system developed in this project.

2. Thematic / Conceptual Framework (Thematic Order)

2.1 Cloud Accounting Adoption in SMEs

Alshamaila, Papagiannidis, and Li (2013) discovered that perceived usefulness, cost-efficiency, and ease of access are the main factors that spur adoption of cloud accounting among SMEs. Their framework emphasizes that SMEs appreciate lower infrastructure expenses and location-free access to financial data. Their research, however, suffers regional limitations through a UK focus. This was extended by Mujalli (2024), who demonstrated that regulatory awareness and vendor trust, in conjunction with organisational readiness, significantly affect adoption behaviour. Even though the study establishes the business value of cloud accounting, it also indicates that SMEs with low digital maturity are still hesitant adopters.

According to OECD (2022), cloud platforms increase the productivity and transparency of SMEs considerably but emphasize that introduction remains unsustainable without policy support and digital education.

Relevance to the project: These findings support building a low-cost, easy-to-use web-based accounting prototype to reduce technological and financial constraints for SMEs.

2.2 Inventory and Accounting Integration

According to Romney and Steinbart (2017), inventory is one of the key accounting drivers affecting cost of goods sold, taxation, and profit reporting. Manual inventory handling often leads to delays in reconciliation and failure to present financial statements correctly.

Liang and Wu (2019) show empirically that automated integration of inventory and accounting records is an important tool to enhance financial accuracy and purchasing efficiency. Their results indicate that real-time stock information improves cash utilisation and minimises stock-outs.

Cin7 (2022) backs these results with industry evidence that automated stock valuation and real-time ledger updates enhance tax reporting and operational planning.

Applicability in this project: These studies are directly relevant to implementing inventory, sales, and accounting modules in FinTrac.

2.3 Receivables and Cash-Flow Management

Atradius (2023) emphasizes that delayed receivables are among the most common factors contributing to liquidity failure in SMEs. The report cites poor monitoring and manual handling of invoices as key causes of payment delay.

Rawashdeh (2023) presents empirically supported evidence that receivable recovery rates and overdue balances improve in SMEs using cloud-based accounting systems. The analysis does not, however, explore user-interface effects on collection efficiency in depth.

Romney and Steinbart (2017) also state that cash-flow forecasting embedded within receivables and payables data is a major improvement in short-term financial planning.

Applicability to this project: FinTrac includes receivables ageing, invoice tracking, and projected cash-flow views aligned with this literature.

2.4 Security, Privacy and Regulatory Compliance

According to Nagahawatta (2021), security is the primary obstacle to cloud adoption by SMEs. Fear of data breach, absence of legal protection, and ambiguity in data ownership discourage migration to cloud platforms.

Chen and Zhu (2020) affirm that perceived security risk has a significant negative impact on adoption intention. Their findings indicate that visible security measures such as encryption, secure authentication, and privacy policies have a strong positive effect on assurance.

Bhimani et al. (2018) argue that financial systems must treat cybersecurity as a minimum feature rather than an optional extra.

Applicability to this project: FinTrac’s architecture applies security-by-design principles (including authentication and data protection appropriate to a prototype deployment).

2.5 Usability and CSV-Based Onboarding

According to Square (2022), usability of new accounting platforms is often low and data migration is challenging, which causes SMEs to abandon new services. Many SMEs still hold historical records in spreadsheets.

According to Cin7 (2022), structured CSV templates with validation and preview reduce onboarding errors. Papa et al. (2018) note that simplified interfaces and domain-specific language can greatly enhance ease of use among non-technical users.

Applicability to this project: FinTrac supports CSV-style data exchange, validation workflows, and simplified dashboards where applicable.

4. Discussion & Gap Identification

The literature shows that cloud accounting can materially enhance financial accuracy, efficiency, and cash-flow visibility for SMEs. Nevertheless, usability requirements of low-tech SMEs in developing economies are not fully served by many commercial systems. Moreover, relatively few solutions combine receivables, inventory, and cash-flow in a single decision-oriented dashboard suited to small businesses. Applied research assessing how simple security design elements affect SME trust remains limited. These gaps motivate a lightweight, integrated prototype such as FinTrac.

5. Conclusion

This literature review has shown that cloud-based accounting and integrated business management systems can benefit SMEs significantly in terms of financial visibility, stock control, and receivables recovery. Adoption is still constrained by usability difficulties, security concerns, and digital skills gaps. FinTrac is positioned to address these limitations through a secure, simplified, CSV-aware accounting prototype tailored to SME needs.
